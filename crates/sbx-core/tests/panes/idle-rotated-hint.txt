  direct descendants of decisions made by Thompson, Ritchie, and their
  collaborators over half a century ago.

  Conclusion

  Unix's history is a remarkable story of how a modest, almost accidental side
  project — built originally so its creators could play a space simulation game
  — grew into one of the most influential pieces of software ever written. Its
  journey through corporate research labs, academic institutions,
  standardization bodies, commercial vendors, and ultimately a global community
  of volunteer open-source developers illustrates how ideas about simplicity,
  modularity, and portability can outlast any single implementation or company.
  Though the specific commercial products called "UNIX" have faded from
  prominence, the philosophy and architecture born in that small room at Bell
  Labs in 1969 continues to run much of the world's computing infrastructure
  today, from smartphones in people's pockets to the servers underpinning the
  modern internet.
